# Invariants (v0.5)

"DB" means a constraint, trigger, index or role grant in `schema.sql` and `roles.sql`. `schema.test.mjs` exercises the application role and every reported regression. `concurrency.test.mjs` uses actual `kernel_login` connections on throwaway Postgres 15 at read committed and repeatable read. Tests use synthetic stored responses, not measured PayPal output. "Kernel" means behavior the application must enforce and test independently.

## Model: a lease
A lease is one buyer approval of a PayPal authorization with cap `cap_minor`. The guaranteed window is the 3-day honor period of the live authorization (`honor_until` equals `created_at` plus 3 days, frozen at insert). After it, PayPal capture is best effort. Reauthorization creates a new PayPal authorization id, allowed only after the honor period, once per root (`max_reauth_depth` = 1) until a logged day-4 test shows a second one works. All ids of one lease form a lineage: one root, one live authorization at a time. The cap applies to the whole lineage.

## Ledger accounts and posting kinds
Debit-normal: HOLD_AVAILABLE, COMMITTED_UNSETTLED, CAPTURED_GROSS (platform cash), PAYPAL_FEES, SELLER_COST, RELEASED, WRITTEN_OFF, CHARGEBACK_LOSS. Credit-normal: BUYER_COMMITMENT, SELLER_PAYABLE. Only these shapes are accepted (`posting_shapes`), one posting per `(ref_type, ref_id)` (clawback is per capture and seller). Every posting must point at a real thing:

| ref_type | Debit | Credit | `ref_id` must be |
|---|---|---|---|
| authorize | HOLD_AVAILABLE x | BUYER_COMMITMENT x | any unique id. x equals the cap |
| commit | COMMITTED_UNSETTLED p | HOLD_AVAILABLE p | a job id (whole job amount) or a served tool call id (its price, same group) |
| capture | CAPTURED_GROSS c, PAYPAL_FEES f | COMMITTED_UNSETTLED c, CAPTURED_GROSS f | a completed capture. c and f equal its row |
| accrue | SELLER_COST s | SELLER_PAYABLE s | a paying decision or served tool call of that seller, s equals its full allocated net after all allocated captures resolve |
| payout | SELLER_PAYABLE s | CAPTURED_GROSS s | a payout row in success with the same amount and seller |
| return | HOLD_AVAILABLE r | COMMITTED_UNSETTLED r | that job's remaining reserve; with a paying decision, only its unpaid portion. Served usage never returns |
| void | RELEASED r | HOLD_AVAILABLE r | any unique id. Needs a succeeded PayPal void call on the lease |
| writeoff | WRITTEN_OFF c | COMMITTED_UNSETTLED c | a declined or denied capture, its full amount, in the same transaction |
| reversal | CHARGEBACK_LOSS r | CAPTURED_GROSS r | a reversed capture. r equals its amount. Cash may go negative here only |
| clawback | SELLER_PAYABLE s | SELLER_COST s | a reversed capture id, capped per seller at accrual attributable to its allocations |

## The invariants
- **I1 Cap across lineage (DB).** Counted captures (intent, pending, completed, unknown, reversed) never exceed `cap_minor`, their authorization's amount, or their decision's amount. Reversed still counts because PayPal does not restore the remainder. The cap is also re-summed at every commit. Captures are inserted only as `intent`, on an active lease, against a live authorization. Every capture has immutable allocations totaling its amount, committed with its intent. Capture status is an allow-list and `declined`, `denied`, `reversed` are terminal.
- **I2 Balanced, single-lease, allowed-shape journal (DB).** Groups balance at commit, belong to one lease and exactly one `(ref_type, ref_id)`, use only allowed shapes, and one reference posts once. A commit and return cannot share a group. Clawbacks also balance per seller, with one posting per capture/seller.
- **I3 Append-only and frozen columns (DB).** `ledger_entries` cannot be updated, deleted or truncated, even by the owner. `decisions` and `verdicts` cannot be updated or deleted even by the owner. The app cannot truncate any table. Money columns, `honor_until`, `expires_at`, tool `price_minor` are immutable. `fee_minor`, `paypal_capture_id`, `paypal_order_id`, `paypal_batch_id`, `call_id`, `processed_at`, and a PayPal call's `response`, `http_status`, `sent_at` are set once and frozen.
- **I4 Bounded accounts (DB).** HOLD_AVAILABLE, COMMITTED_UNSETTLED, CAPTURED_GROSS never go below zero (CAPTURED_GROSS only through a reversal). SELLER_PAYABLE never goes below zero per seller.
- **I5 One money call and one capture in flight per lease (DB).** One capture, reauthorize or void call in intent, sent or unknown. One capture in intent, pending or unknown. One capture per call.
- **I6 Intent before call (DB for captures, Kernel for the rest).** A capture row needs its own open capture call row. Kernel rule: commit intent rows in their own transaction before the HTTP call. Never hold the PayPal call inside the ledger transaction.
- **I7 Unknown resolves by reading (DB plus Kernel).** A sent call becomes succeeded or failed only with a stored PayPal response (a GET result for a lost response). A sent call cannot be marked failed without a stored response.
- **I8 Authorization lineage and clock (DB).** One root, one live authorization, one child per parent, parent in the same lease, depth at most `max_reauth_depth`. Amount is exactly cap minus counted captures. Timestamps must agree with the clock: `created_at` within `created_at_slack` of now (1 minute) and never in the future, `honor_until` = `created_at` + 3 days, `expires_at` after the honor period and within 29 days. A child is created at least 3 days after its parent and before the parent's expiry. Status is an allow-list. `voided` needs a succeeded void call. `superseded` and every child need a succeeded reauthorize call on the parent lease. Authorizations start created. `expired` needs the actual expiry clock or a succeeded GET with matching id and EXPIRED status. `denied` needs a succeeded matching GET/authorize response. `captured` requires completed/reversed captures totaling the authorization amount.
- **I9 Capture needs committed usage and stays within its decision (DB).** In-flight captures never exceed COMMITTED_UNSETTLED. Each capture allocates to its own lease's paying jobs or served tool calls. A decision-tied capture allocates only to its own job. Across all captures, per-ref allocations (including declined allocations consumed by writeoff) plus returns never exceed the ref commit. Job allocations never exceed its decision amount. Monetary job/tool and decision/tool references cannot collide on a lease.
- **I10 The database derives the amount (DB).** A job decision equals floor(job amount x pay_bps / 10000), where pay_bps is the rubric band for the lowest passing score of the latest verdict round, and `derived_from` records that score and pay_bps. A rubric needs distinct min_score values and pay_bps that never decrease as the score rises. A result of 0 can only be a void.
- **I11 Disagreement holds, it does not void (DB).** Latest round, distinct allow-listed providers: two passes and nothing else pay the derived amount. Two fails and nothing else void. A failed pre-check voids only when `derived_from.precheck_failed` is JSON `true` and there are no verdicts at all. Everything else, including one unsure, one fail or a missing judge, can only hold. Each round accepts exactly two distinct providers. A hold opens a new round (up to 3) only once the current round is complete and neither unanimous pass nor unanimous fail. A hold taken while a judge is missing does not override the later completed result. Unanimous passes freeze further verdicts. Verdict and decision inserts lock the lease then the job; decisions record the derived round (0 for no verdicts). A deferred final-decision check forbids verdicts in later rounds.
- **I12 Webhooks (DB plus Kernel).** The key is (event id, signature_verified), so an attacker's unverified event E cannot block PayPal's verified event E: both rows coexist and only the verified one can be processed. A repeat delivery increments `deliveries` by exactly one by upsert. Payload, signature flag and type cannot change. An unverified event can never be marked processed. State changes come from API reads, not payload fields.
- **I13 Payouts (DB).** Payouts start queued. One non-failed payout per decision, one payout per call and unique non-null batch id. Each payout is bounded by its own decision or metered source accrual, less attributable clawbacks and prior non-failed payouts. A failed row can be replaced. Queue cancellation accepts no call, an intent call or a failed call. At send, the call must be payout/same lease with the exact amount and be sent or succeeded; the payout must be due, unheld, its job unheld, its source still funded and captured cash sufficient after outstanding sent/unknown payouts. Reversed captures freeze payouts until their attributable accrued amounts have clawbacks. At success, the call must succeed with matching `batch_header.payout_batch_id`, exact two-decimal `batch_header.amount.value` and batch_status SUCCESS; full payout posting is required in that same transaction. Receiver is copied and immutable. The seller hold is at least 30 seconds and cannot decrease. Jobs going held set queued payouts' held_at; only held to decided clears it, through the nested jobs trigger. Direct held_at edits are refused. A held payout can be cancelled.
- **I14 Close (DB).** A lease closes only when every completed allocation has its full attributed net accrued, HOLD_AVAILABLE zero, COMMITTED_UNSETTLED zero, no unresolved PayPal call, no in-flight capture and no live authorization. Lease state is an allow-list. A closed lease accepts only payout, reversal and clawback postings and no captures.
- **I15 Reconcile equals PayPal (Kernel plus Proof page).** For every lease, our captures and authorization status equal PayPal's, read by API. Differences are shown, never auto-fixed silently.
- **I16 Conservation (DB, under lease lock).** BUYER_COMMITMENT = HOLD_AVAILABLE + COMMITTED_UNSETTLED + RELEASED + WRITTEN_OFF + total captured, never above the cap. A lease goes active only with an authorize posting equal to the cap and exactly one live authorization.
- **I17 Ledger equals PayPal records (DB, under lease lock).** A capture completes only when its call succeeded and the stored response shows the same id, amount and fee. Amount and fee must be two-decimal strings; no rounding of longer fractions. Declined/denied needs its call failed with a stored response, or succeeded with status DECLINED/DENIED, plus a full atomic writeoff. Completed and reversed captures equal the capture postings and fees. Reversed captures equal reversal postings. Every posting points at the thing in the table above. A void posting needs a succeeded void call.
- **I18 Exposure and honor window (DB).** A commit is refused when COMMITTED_UNSETTLED would exceed `unsettled_limit_minor` or when there is no live authorization inside its honor period.

## Capture allocation and net rules

`capture_allocations(capture_id, lease_id, ref_id, amount_minor)` is append-only. Insert every allocation with the capture intent. `ref_id` is the job id or served tool call id, never a decision id. A null capture decision supports batching jobs and tool calls across sellers. Job accrual still uses the paying decision id; tool accrual uses the served call id.

`allocation_nets` distributes capture net in ref_id order using cumulative integer floors: floor(net * cumulative gross / gross) minus the previous cumulative floor. Every cent is assigned once. `allocation_earnings` attributes each ref's accrual across its captures in capture_id order with the same rule. `allocation_recoveries` attributes a capture/seller clawback back to those sources, so a job cannot spend metered earnings.

Conservative single-accrual rule: finish all allocations and captures for a ref before accruing its full known net in one posting. No later capture allocation for that ref is allowed. Pending/unknown/intent or reversed allocated captures prevent a new accrual. Declined allocations consume their ref's commit through the mandatory full writeoff. Accrue is allowed on expired and failed leases when the captures completed. Close has no platform-remainder escape and refuses any unpaid completed allocation.

All state-changing kernel transactions must take the lease lock first, including job appeal and payout updates, then the job lock when needed. Retry the whole transaction on 40001. Commit call and payout sent state together before external HTTP; do not dispatch from an unvalidated queued row.

## Rules the database cannot enforce
- **Isolation.** Run at READ COMMITTED. Every write path takes the lease lock through `lock_lease()`, a real row update (`schema.test.mjs` checks the function body still does). Under REPEATABLE READ it fails with 40001 (tested), so the kernel retries whole transactions on 40001.
- **search_path.** Every function pins `search_path = pg_catalog, pg_temp` and qualifies every table and function. The application role has no TEMP privilege. Connect as kernel_login (LOGIN, NOSUPERUSER, NOINHERIT), then SET ROLE pashadhara_app on each connection. RESET ROLE returns to kernel_login, which has no schema privileges. Provision its password from env during deployment, never in repository SQL. Never run the kernel as the table owner or a superuser: an owner can disable triggers, replace functions and truncate.
- **Settings.** `created_at_slack` and `max_reauth_depth` live in `kernel_settings`, writable only by the owner.
- **Declined or denied capture.** If the usage was served, record PayPal decline evidence and the full `writeoff` with the capture status change atomically. A locally failed unsent intent is not decline evidence.
- **Reversal.** Post the status change and the full reversal in one transaction. Post clawback per capture/seller for attributable unpaid accrual. Payouts remain frozen until that accrual is covered. A queued payout whose source was clawed back cannot send from another source. If the seller was already paid, a clawback cannot make payable negative; recovery requires an operator and is not invented as cash. The negative CAPTURED_GROSS is the real platform loss (the seller was paid and the money came back).
- **Held jobs.** A complete disagreement round can be re-judged in a new round. An appeal is withdrawn or upheld through held to decided, which clears queued payout holds. Held to judging retains them. A job that stays held is settled by the buyer cancelling the reserve (post `return`). No operator override exists in this contract.
- **Platform-wide exposure.** `unsettled_limit_minor` is per lease. A cap across all leases needs a global lock and is not enforced.
- **Judge safety.** Quote the deliverable as untrusted data, run deterministic pre-checks first, and never insert a verdict whose pre-check failed. The database bounds the amount and the verdict combinations but cannot tell a fooled judge from an honest one.
- **Fee source.** `fee_minor` must equal the fee in PayPal's stored response, so the kernel cannot choose it. The response is written by the application role, so this binds the books to what the kernel recorded, and the Proof page's independent GET is what checks that against PayPal.
- **Fault injection routes** exist only against the PayPal sandbox with `FAULTS_ENABLED=true`, behind the admin credential.

## Not guaranteed (state this in the product)
- Funds after the 3-day honor period. A capture may be declined. `declined` and `denied` are normal terminal states.
- A wrong release that two judges agree on. The database pays at most the rubric amount. The payout hold window and buyer appeal limit the damage. Chargeback risk sits with the platform as merchant of record.
